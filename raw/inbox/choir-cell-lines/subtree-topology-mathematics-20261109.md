## What can be counted

Fix a leaf set S of size m, with distinct labels. A rooted binary topology here is unranked and non-plane. Its children are unordered, all internal vertices are unlabelled, and there are no branch lengths in the count. Starting from m−1 leaves, subdivide one of the 2m−4 edges or insert above the root and attach the new leaf. Deleting that particular leaf reverses the operation. The recurrence gives (2m−3)!! rooted topologies. For a fixed root split L|R there are (2|L|−3)!!(2|R|−3)!! completions, using (−1)!!=1 for singleton sides. Summing over all possible unordered root splits gives the same total. An unrooted binary topology on m≥3 leaves has (2m−5)!! possibilities and m−3 internal edges. Root insertion has 2m−3 edge choices and recovers the rooted count. [Felsenstein, The Number of Evolutionary Trees](https://doi.org/10.2307/2412810)

| Object | Number possible on fixed S | Number in one binary topology |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Unordered nonempty root cut L|R, with L union R = S | 2^(m−1)−1 | 1 |
| Rooted proper nonsingleton clade C as a subset of S | 2^m−m−2 | m−2 |
| Nontrivial unrooted edge split of S, both sides size at least 2 | 2^(m−1)−m−1 | m−3 |
| Unordered local sibling pair anywhere, with the rest of S allowed outside the parent | (3^m−2·2^m+1)/2 | m−1 |

The last count follows from assigning each leaf to left, right or outside, excluding assignments with an empty child, and dividing by two. It counts possible hypotheses over variable unions, not the comparisons actually visited by a gate. A rooted clade is an oriented subset, while an unrooted split identifies the subset and its complement. Complementary root children induce the same edge split when the degree-two root is suppressed. For four leaves, Wolfram gives 15 rooted topologies, seven unordered nonempty root cuts, three nontrivial unrooted splits, and ten proper nonsingleton clades. Rooted clades are laminar. The two root cuts AB|CD and AC|BD cross, with all four intersections nonempty, so one unrooted tree cannot contain both as edge splits. A clustering linkage imposes its own rooted hierarchy on the data, and need not produce a tree whose branch-path distances exactly reproduce the input distances. Additive tree-metric reconstruction has a separate four-point condition. [Buneman, A Note on the Metric Properties of Trees](https://doi.org/10.1016/0095-8956(74)90047-1)

For m points in affine general position in effective dimension k, Cover's homogeneous dichotomy formula after affine lifting gives `sum_{i=0}^k binom(m−1,i)−1` strict nonempty affine cuts, counting complements once. Remove the empty-side cut. Degeneracy can reduce the number. The effective dimension is the affine dimension of the projected points, which can be less than the number of input columns. This is a bound for cuts of a fixed cloud and remains a geometric count when the cloud is random. It does not count linkage trees, prove cut-specific probability bounds, or apply to cuts created by arbitrarily breaking threshold ties. [Cover, Theorem 1](https://isl.stanford.edu/~cover/papers/paper2.pdf)

## What subtree spectra and distances identify

Write Y for the centered m×p subtree matrix, V for retained orthonormal feature directions and Z=YV. The squared Euclidean distance matrix D² is determined by the row Gram K=ZZ', and K=−J D² J/2 where J=I−11'/m. Feature covariance eigenvalues fix the singular values, without fixing the labeled row Gram. Thus the spectrum alone cannot determine distances, possible cuts or linkage topology.

- For labeled coordinates x=(0,1,2,10), average linkage has tied nearest pairs AB and BC. The selected pair subsequently joins the remaining one of A,B,C before D, giving root ABC|D and two possible rooted refinements under different initial tie conventions. For y=(0,1,9,10), nearest disjoint pairs AB and CD both merge before any cross-pair, giving root AB|CD. Centering and scaling each configuration to sample variance one gives the same rank and sole eigenvalue. Wolfram checked the distances and variances. The x example also shows that a simple nonzero eigenvalue does not preclude a distance tie or multiple tie-compatible hierarchies.
- More generally, every labeled rooted binary topology can be realized by average linkage on a line. Embed each child recursively in its own short interval and separate the two child intervals by a gap larger than both diameters. Before the root merge, every average distance between active clusters within a child is smaller than every cross-child average distance. Both children therefore complete their own hierarchy before the root merge. A final center/rescale gives the same chosen positive one-dimensional variance across all these clouds. A fixed general-position line cloud still has only m−1 strict threshold cuts.
- For the square with rows A=(-1,-1), B=(1,-1), C=(-1,1), D=(1,1), the covariance with divisor four is identity. Using the whole tied two-dimensional eigenspace preserves every pairwise Euclidean distance under a basis change. Keeping only the first coordinate can produce AC|BD, whereas keeping only the second gives AB|CD. Whole-block geometry is basis invariant. Choosing one vector within the block changes the question, and a large number of possible vectors need not give equally many distinct cuts.

Use the subtree's cells and an explicit definition of its geometry when generating candidates. Rebuilding a graph and diffusion on S, restricting existing global diffusion coordinates, and using distances in a subtree PCA subspace can give different answers. They are distinct deterministic algorithms to replay. Specify centering, feature metric, retained blocks, linkage, exact tie convention, minimum leaf sizes and the set of candidate methods. In a repeated eigenspace, candidate directions may be defined through the projector or scanned through a prespecified rule. A numerical near-tie needs a declared threshold or a stability analysis. Distance ties, eigenvalue ties, and similar scores for crossing cuts are different phenomena.

A bounded alternative search can include different linkages in the same geometry, a fixed list of geometry choices, or local rearrangements of an existing subtree hierarchy. Deduplicate candidates by their canonical labeled clades when comparing whole topologies, or by complementary cut identities when comparing root cuts. A clade-set symmetric difference measures structural disagreement with the baseline on the same leaves. It is a descriptive statistic until a reference experiment is specified. Scanning directional thresholds gives candidate root cuts and needs an additional completion rule to give full topologies. If each candidate topology is allowed to choose its own best cut, the maximization and the topology completion rule both belong in the replay.

## Which distances identify a topology

A fixed labeled distance matrix and fixed deterministic linkage, including its tie-breaking rule, give one computed topology. To count ambiguity, specify the family being counted. With exact tie branching, at each active-cluster state recurse over every minimizer of the linkage criterion and deduplicate terminal trees by their labeled clades. Different merge histories can give the same unranked tree. No tie at any reached decision implies one history, but distinct initial distances alone are insufficient. For Ward on the line at (0,1,3,3+5/sqrt(3)), the unique first merge joins 0 and 1. Its Ward distance to 3 then ties the distance between the last two points, both squared 25/3. A deterministic near-tie tolerance defines a different search rule. Branching over near-minimizers state by state does not establish that every result is realizable by a single perturbation of the input metric. An uncertainty region for the original distance matrix can define that latter family, with coverage depending on how the region was obtained. [Ward, minimum-variance criterion](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.1963.10500845)

For the square above, four sides tie at squared distance 4. After merging a side, the opposite side is uniquely next at squared distance 4, compared with 20/3 from the merged side to either singleton. There are four merge histories and two distinct rooted topologies. For an exactly equidistant regular simplex of m leaves, the Ward update preserves equality because the numerator coefficients sum to their denominator. Every pair remains eligible at every stage. All (2m−3)!! rooted topologies occur and there are m!(m−1)!/2^(m−1) ranked merge histories. At m=4 these counts are 15 and 18. Exact equidistance to two points or centroids under a metric establishes an ambiguity for that comparison. It need not produce a tie for Ward's size-weighted minimum among the active clusters. Small distance margins measure local sensitivity, and require a perturbation model to become statistical evidence. [Wolfram capture](subtree-topology-mathematics-20261109-wolfram.json)

For several specified subspaces, compute each output family on the same labels. The union gives topologies possible in at least one view, the intersection gives topologies possible in every view, and the union size requires deduplication. An intersection of supported clades or unrooted splits can describe a common partial hierarchy even if the whole-tree intersection is empty. Crossing splits cannot both occur as edge splits of one unrooted tree, and crossing clades cannot both occur in a rooted hierarchy. An empty whole-tree intersection can therefore reflect genuinely crossed structure, such as a row factor and a column factor, without establishing that one view is erroneous.

When subspaces V_j are mutually orthogonal and their nonnegative weights w_j are fixed, the product Euclidean metric obeys `d_w²(i,l)=sum_j w_j ||V_j'(x_i−x_l)||²`. Equal weights over a complete orthogonal decomposition recover full-space Euclidean distance. Overlapping or cumulative spans cannot be treated as independent pieces of that decomposition without double counting. On the square, greater weight for the first coordinate yields AC|BD under Ward, greater weight for the second yields AB|CD, and equal positive weights give the two exact-tie outputs. Fixed weights answer which topology an algorithm returns under a specified metric. A choice of scientifically correct metric requires a target or a generative assumption.

There are settings in which distances do identify a tree. If an exact metric is additive on a leaf-labeled tree, Buneman's four-point condition characterizes it. On a resolved quartet ab|cd with internal edge length l>0, the two cross sums tie and exceed d(a,b)+d(c,d) by 2l. A uniform distance error smaller than l/2 preserves which sum is strictly smallest. This identifies the quartet split, and an additive metric with positive internal edges identifies the reduced unrooted topology. It does not locate a root. An exact ultrametric satisfies d(a,c)≤max(d(a,b),d(b,c)); its nested threshold classes identify a rooted dendrogram with heights, allowing simultaneous merges and leaving arbitrary zero-height-difference binary refinements unidentified. Both are claims under structural distance assumptions, not automatic properties of the observed projected distances or guarantees for Ward. The number of compatible algorithm outputs, the number of trees within a statistical uncertainty set, and the uniquely identifiable population topology are three different targets. [Buneman, four-point condition](https://doi.org/10.1016/0095-8956(74)90047-1) · [Carlsson and Mémoli, dendrogram–ultrametric equivalence](https://jmlr.csail.mit.edu/papers/volume11/carlsson10a/carlsson10a.pdf)

## Bootstrap, permutation and conditional rotation

An ordinary nonparametric bootstrap resamples the chosen sampling units. Its split frequency describes recurrence under that perturbation scheme. Resampling cells creates repeats and omits leaf identities, so it requires a matching rule on shared leaves, or a separate projected-label task, to discuss the same split. Resampling genes or characters can preserve the leaves, but presumes a defensible unit and dependence model. Classical phylogenetic bootstrap keeps taxa and resamples characters, under its character-sampling assumptions. A fitted parametric bootstrap can impose a null and replay an entire search, with calibration dependent on the model and estimation. A bootstrap distribution centered on the observed empirical law generally carries the observed signal into the resamples. Shimodaira's AU procedure is a specific multiscale correction for testing regions with selection, with its own assumptions and target. Its existence does not turn split recurrence into a Gaussian sibling p-value. [Efron, Bootstrap Methods](https://doi.org/10.1214/aos/1176344552) · [Felsenstein, Confidence Limits on Phylogenies](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1558-5646.1985.tb00420.x) · [Shimodaira, AU Test](https://doi.org/10.1080/10635150290069913)

Permuting exchangeable cell rows relative to external fixed groups can give a valid fixed-group test. For an unsupervised tree that is equivariant to relabeling, permuting complete rows and replaying the whole search simply relabels its leaves. A label-invariant maximum or topology-comparison score then stays constant. Permuting features or residuals requires the corresponding separate invariance. Under the matrix-normal common-mean Gaussian null, orthogonal rotations in the centered row subspace preserve the feature Gram and change the row geometry, providing a different and useful exact conditional reference.

## Fixed-set Beta, global replay, and selected-subtree Haar tests

For a fixed union S under independent N_p(mu,Σ) rows with arbitrary common positive-definite Σ, choose an orthonormal matrix U whose columns span the subspace orthogonal to the ones vector in S. Let Y be the centered data and G=Y'Y. Conditional on G and the union mean, the centered data in U coordinates are uniform on the row-orthogonal orbit with Gram G. The same holds conditional on all rows outside S, whose Gaussian means may differ, under independent rows with a shared covariance. The orbit is a Stiefel manifold in the rank-r factorization `U'Y=Q diag(s) V_r'` for r=rank(G). Use a factorization determined by G. The left rotations preserve the Gaussian density on this orbit, and the orthogonal group acts transitively, yielding its Haar probability. This includes the ordinary full-column-rank case in Chen–Gao. A rank-deficient orbit has a corresponding rank-r factorization, but an inverse-covariance statistic on the full saturated subspace may be undefined or constant. [Chen and Gao, Sections 3.1–3.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.17458v1)

For a fixed two-way split of S with normalized centered indicator u and a Gram-measurable V of rank k in the data, set `R²=u'Proj(col(YV))u`. For 0<k<m−1 its conditional law is Beta(k/2,(m−1−k)/2). If H projects onto col(YV), Haar invariance makes the squared length of H u a ratio of independent chi-square sums. Equivalently `(m−1−k)R²/[k(1−R²)]` has F(k,m−1−k). Conditioning on G permits rank selection from G, including a feature-spectrum threshold with Gram-only tie conventions. Selecting the rank from u and the row orientation invalidates the naïve Beta reference. At k=m−1, all nonempty cuts have R²=1. The Beta survival tail is a reference for R² and the equivalent Hotelling statistic. It is not the analytic reference for the existing sibling score with its adaptive contrast projection, covariance guards and branch adjustment. An all-label Bonferroni bound for R² at a fixed union uses deterministic split labels and is valid under the Gaussian global union null, conditional on G and averaged over the selected split. A fixed finite label family with different Gram-measurable V_j can also use each cut's own rank-specific Beta p-value in Bonferroni. The realized Cover count cannot substitute for a deterministic label family. [Chen and Gao, Proposition 3.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.17458v1)

For a fixed union that was declared before selection, a global Gaussian test of a recomputed score can draw independent Haar orientations conditional on G and the mean. In each replay generate candidates and run the entire procedure. Rank the observed statistic among the observed and replayed statistics. With B independent replays, the weak upper rank `(1+# replay scores >= observed)/(B+1)` is superuniform, with a minimum of 1/(B+1). This validates a fixed-union global null, including the choice of split when the replay also searches. It does not condition on a specific split or on selecting that union from a larger tree.

For a data-selected subtree or sibling pair, condition on outside rows, the observed mean and Gram of the selected union S, and a discrete event E under which the full selection procedure would return the target. One must replay how the subtree became eligible from the full data. If testing a fixed selected pair, E includes returning the pair. If testing the best alternative inside the selected subtree, E may include only selecting that subtree and reaching it, with candidate generation and maximization inside the test statistic. If membership was determined by an upstream fit, rebuilding only the induced subtree and dropping that upstream event does not justify inference. A baseline topology used as a frozen input must either be predeclared or included in E when selected. Do not constrain continuous fitted branch times to equal their observed values just by exact equality. Conditional probability of a generic exact fitted value is zero and would need a separate disintegration. Under the common Gaussian mean null for all rows in S, the resulting law is Haar restricted to E. This is the strong common-mean model. Equality of the two average observation means alone allows other nonzero centered row means in S and does not yield that Haar law with arbitrary unknown Σ. [Chen and Gao, Remark 2.2 and Section 3.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.17458v1)

A symmetric data-independent proposal on the row orbit, rejected back to its current state when E fails, is reversible for the restricted Haar target. A Givens rotation proposal can use a symmetric distribution on angles and coordinate planes. Include reflection moves if needed to connect orientation components in square full-row rank. Connectivity affects attainable power, while reversibility and initialization under the restricted null supply the rank validity. For the serial Besag–Clifford construction, choose J uniformly in 0..M and put the observed orientation at position J. Run the same reversible transition forward and backward to obtain the full path. By detailed balance the joint path law given any J is its stationary Markov path law, so J is uniform conditional on that path. The weak upper rank divided by M+1 is superuniform. A uniform random tie ordering gives exact discrete ranks. Do not use an ordinary forward-chain exceedance fraction as an exact p-value, or choose the kernel, step size or M from the observed orientation or scores. A prespecified mixture or tuning based only on the conditioned quantities is allowed. Null failures due to a numerical exception should be retained separately, rather than automatically treated as legitimate out-of-event proposals. [Besag and Clifford, Generalized Monte Carlo Significance Tests](https://doi.org/10.1093/biomet/76.4.633) · [Chen and Gao, Proposition 4.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.17458v1)

The path rank theorem permits any prespecified deterministic real-valued function of each replayed dataset. Therefore one can retain the production sibling statistic and replace its reference by the conditional rank. This is a consequence of the rank proof. Chen–Gao develop their own Hotelling statistic and their analytic shortcuts do not establish the event geometry for diffusion, NNLS or traversal. Recompute all data-dependent pieces used by the retained score and by E, including the appropriate covariance, contrast rank and branch time. Include eligibility or reference-weight inputs when they affect E or the statistic. Freeze external random seeds consistently or explicitly include the randomization in the statistical experiment. Holding observed data-dependent quantities fixed without an argument changes the test. Holding the observed whole row-distance matrix fixed can eliminate variation of a distance-only statistic. Exactness under Gaussian rows also does not extend automatically to normalized counts, unequal cell-depth variation or a learned embedding whose law is unspecified.

## Which statistic answers each question

| Question | Statistic and calibration target |
| --- | --- |
| Is a predeclared fixed cut extreme on a fixed union? | Beta survival for its R² under the common Gaussian union null, or Haar replay of the retained sibling score. |
| Is the observed selected cut extreme given the selection? | Keep its identities fixed in the score, include its selection in E, and use the restricted Haar rank. |
| Is a named alternative topology stronger than the baseline under the homogeneous null? | Declare a deterministic scalar topology score F, for example an aggregate over corresponding cuts. Replay the signed difference F(alternative)−F(baseline) on the same orientation. A two-sided departure needs a prespecified direction-free statistic. The resulting p-value is evidence against the homogeneous null for that contrast. It does not test equality of two topology methods' expected quality under arbitrary distributions. |
| Is any alternative cut or topology contrast extreme after searching? | Freeze the candidate generation, baseline rule, eligibility, score, comparability across sizes/ranks, and empty-family convention, then recompute the same maximum on each replay. A model-based joint maximum accounts for dependence under the tested global null. |
| Are many individual discoveries valid across different true/false subtrees? | Requires a separate multiple-testing argument under the relevant local nulls and their dependence/selection. Global maximum calibration gives weak familywise control under the complete null. It does not by itself give strong FWER, FDR, or final-partition control. |

A comparison can score different topologies on the same cells and the same replicate orientation. This preserves the paired dependence. Two separate marginal p-values do not estimate the tail of their difference. When topology candidates, directions or a best threshold were chosen by optimizing observed data, the winning observed candidate alone cannot be inserted into an otherwise fixed-family reference. Either replay the selection with the joint statistic or condition on the selection event that produced it. The scope of the null and the selection target determine which one is appropriate.
