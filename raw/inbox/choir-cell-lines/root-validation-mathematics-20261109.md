## A, B and C test different hypotheses

| Construction | Intended null and actual selection | Statistic and reference |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A, production projected-Wald | Fixed-set contrast null: the two sets' average observation means coincide. In use, the tree returns both children, branch times and covariance are fitted, the projection dimension can depend on the contrast, and eligible frontiers are selected in traversal. The reference does not condition on this event. | For continuous coordinates and the relevant whitening branch, `z = covariance^(-1/2) (mean_L - mean_R) / sqrt((1/n_L + 1/n_R) g)`, with `g = 1 + b/(2 mean_b)`. The score is squared projected contrast energy. The reference is central chi-square with the selected dimension, scaled by the fitted empirical-null factor when eligible. |
| B, ceiling 3 and flat-cut count | One population with an intended bound of 3 on the best-cut population ratio. The parent feature covariance determines invariant directions and rank. No observed topology or edge selection is conditioned on. | `rho = pi(1-pi) delta' S_w^(-1) delta`. B compares `n rho` with noncentral chi-square of rank `k` and noncentrality `3n`, then multiplies by the Cover count and caps at one. |
| C, best-cut EC | A specified one-population law with a known population optimum and known field covariance would permit a best-cut reference. C supplies uniform boundary constants even for ranks where that model does not exist. It uses B's geometry and rank and does not condition on tree selection. | Exact transform `R² = (n rho/(n-2))/(1+n rho/(n-2))`. C compares `sqrt(n)(R-R*)/sigma_R` with a monotone envelope of the expected Euler characteristic of an even Gaussian field. |

Equal underlying row means on two fixed sets concern the observations' mean parameters. The conditional means of a one-population distribution after a feature threshold can differ, even if every observation has the same underlying mean parameter. For instance, a standard Gaussian sign cut has conditional means ±sqrt(2/pi). Conversely two components can share a mean and differ in covariance or shape. The stronger null of a common Gaussian mean and common covariance for every row of the tested union implies fixed-set mean equality. Rejecting it rejects that model, with no identification of a component count. An unrestricted one-population class of iid laws includes mixture distributions and cannot, by itself, distinguish a population from a mixture representation of the same law. [Gao, Bien and Witten, selective mean contrast](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.02936) · [Chen and Gao, common-mean null and Remark 2.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.17458v1)

## What scaling can correct

Dividing the score by a fixed scale gives an exact central chi-square reference when the projected Gaussian covariance is a scalar multiple of identity and that scalar is accounted for, with fixed rank and a correctly fixed projection or appropriate independence. A fixed branch multiplier simply changes this scalar under that model. With fixed projected Gaussian covariance eigenvalues `lambda_j`, the score is a weighted sum of independent chi-square-one variates. Matching the mean using `c = sum(lambda_j)/d` matches the variance only if all eigenvalues are equal, by Cauchy–Schwarz. A covariance estimate from the same two independent Gaussian groups yields a Hotelling F reference in the ordinary fixed-group pooled-covariance setup. A fitted branch multiplier, within-child/parent fallback, and contrast-dependent dimension each require their own argument.

A scalar cannot generally restore the shape changed by selection. For example, if the rule chooses the maximum of two independent chi-square-d scores, the CDF at zero has exponent d rather than d/2, which any positive scale leaves unchanged. The upper tail retains the exponential rate of the original score, so allowing a different reference degrees of freedom and a scale cannot match both endpoints either. For a regular data-derived population cut with positive limiting projected mean contrast, stable within covariance, `pi` bounded away from zero and one, and bounded branch multiplier, the unscaled score has order n. If `T_n/n -> s > 0`, then `T_n/c_n` diverges when `c_n=o(n)`, converges to a point when `c_n/n` converges to a positive constant, and vanishes if `c_n/n -> infinity`. The order-n shift and order-sqrt(n) fluctuations cannot both be reproduced by a single deterministic or consistently estimated scale. An alpha-specific calibrated threshold can be written as a scale algebraically, but requires the selected-null quantile already. The existing family/context weighting can improve empirical agreement on a panel and does not prove that reference.

## Legitimate cut geometry and the scope of B and C

With both sides nonempty and positive-definite `S_w`, rho is the maximum scalar between/within ratio over linear directions. Take an attaining direction for any partition, sort its projected observations, and choose the same number of observations at the end that maximizes the absolute difference of means. Total projected variance stays fixed and between variance increases, so within variance decreases and the ratio cannot fall. Without threshold ties this gives a strictly separable affine cut. At ties the weak cut may need a tie rule. Coincident points or singular within covariance need explicit treatment, and the quotient `R²` remains well-defined using the projector onto the centered feature span even when the inverse-based rho saturates.

A full binary hierarchy on n leaves has n−1 internal nodes and one sibling comparison per internal node. At any given fixed n-element set there are `2^(n−1)−1` unordered nonempty splits. In affine general position in dimension k there are `sum(j=0..k) binom(n−1,j) − 1` strictly separable nonempty splits after identifying complements. This is an upper count under degeneracy and becomes the full count for k≥n−1. The realized family depends on the observations. A union bound requires a deterministic family or valid conditional tails for the realized family. A tree count, a realized flat-cut count and a deterministic all-label count refer to different families. [Cover, Theorem 1](https://isl.stanford.edu/~cover/papers/paper2.pdf)

The population ratio is bounded by 3 at a scalar **mode cut** of a finite-variance unimodal law, using Khintchine's mixture-of-uniform-segments representation. The denominator slack is `[pi a − (1−pi)b]^2`, where a and b are the conditional mean distances from the mode. A Gaussian projected population also has the threshold-specific bound in `cut_rule.py` and maximum `2/(pi_math−2)`, about 1.752, over all thresholds. Both statements concern population ratios. For a non-mode threshold the continuous, decreasing density `0.9 U(0,0.001)+0.1 U(0,1)` gives exact rho `1367002729/184734185 = 7.3998363053` at 1/3. Sending the narrow component width to zero and then the tail mass to zero gives `3(1−t)(1+t)^2/(1−3t+3t²+3t³)`. Wolfram verified its maximum 8 at t=1/3 because the numerator slack from 8 factors as `(3t−1)^2(3t+5)`. This is a lower bound on any putative ceiling over all unimodal laws and arbitrary cuts. For B at fixed k, the log Cover count is O(log n), whereas its noncentral chi-square upper tail at `n(3+epsilon)` decays exponentially by the Gaussian representation. Therefore B rejects with probability tending to one if the cut statistic consistently converges to any rho above 3. [Khintchine representation, Ion, Klaassen and van den Heuvel, Appendix A.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2208.08813v2) · [B implementation](../../../results/analyses/gaussian_cut_rule_20261108/cut_rule.py) · [Wolfram capture](../../../results/analyses/root_validation_followup_20261109/math/wolfram.json)

For two Gaussian components with known true labels, common within-component covariance Σ, and limiting left share 0<pi<1, define the standardized mean distance in the retained coordinates by `Delta² = (mu_L−mu_R)' Σ^(-1) (mu_L−mu_R)`. At that true-label split the population ratio is exactly `rho=pi(1−pi)Delta²`. Thus the true-label ratio strictly exceeds 3 exactly when `Delta>sqrt(3/[pi(1−pi)])`. Wolfram gives thresholds of 3.464, 4.330 and 7.947 for left shares 0.50, 0.20 and 0.05. For fixed positive k, convergence to a value strictly above the boundary drives B/C's raw tail scores to zero. A value below 3 misses the population criterion. Equality needs separate boundary analysis. These are statements about the true-label population geometry and raw rules. A selected tree can cut elsewhere, omit informative directions or close at a gate. The relationship alone does not establish the cause of the four-component outcomes. ## Exact Gaussian root benchmark

Let Y be the centered n×p data matrix at a fixed root. For a deterministic nonempty split let `u=(1_L−pi 1)/sqrt(n pi(1−pi))`. Let V be the p×k matrix of feature directions, and `H=Proj(col(YV))`. Then the preceding R² is exactly `u' H u`, because `(YV)'u = sqrt(n pi(1−pi)) delta` and `S_T=(YV)'(YV)/n`.

Suppose the rows before centering are independent Gaussian with one common mean and common positive-definite feature covariance. In a fixed basis of 1-perpendicular, the centered data have a matrix-normal law invariant under any orthogonal transformation of the n−1 row coordinates. Conditional on the feature Gram `G=Y'Y`, the orbit of possible centered data is transitive under that group, and the conditional law is its unique invariant probability. Any retained V chosen as a deterministic function of G with `rank(YV)=k` gives a Haar rank-k projector H conditional on G. This includes a Gram-only rank threshold and arbitrary fixed tie convention depending on G, or retaining a full tied block. If the tie convention uses the contrast or individual row orientations the argument does not apply. For 0<k<n−1, each fixed u has exactly `R²|G ~ Beta(k/2,(n−1−k)/2)`: in a Haar frame the squared projection is the sum of the first k squared standard normals divided by the sum of all n−1. Then `[(n−1−k)/k] R²/(1−R²) = [(n−1−k)/(k(n−2))] n rho ~ F(k,n−1−k)`. This remains true at random Gram-only k conditionally, including an MP-only cutoff if its geometry meets these conditions. It is distinct from A's contrast-dependent rank and branch scaling. At k=0 there is no projected contrast. At k=n−1 every nonempty split gives R²=1 and pooled inversion for that full subspace fails. The production n≤p+1 guard avoids using the saturated full space and also skips some potentially informative lower-dimensional cases. [Chen and Gao, Proposition 3.2 and Appendix A.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.17458v1) · [Wolfram capture](../../../results/analyses/root_validation_followup_20261109/math/wolfram.json)

For any root split chosen from those n cells by any algorithm, `min(1,(2^(n−1)−1) BetaSF(R²_obs))` is superuniform under this Gaussian global null, conditional on G and averaged over selection. The proof unions over a deterministic label family and applies even when the algorithm uses other data-dependent quantities to choose the split, so long as the null for Y holds and V for the score is the same Gram-based subspace for every split. It is not a p-value conditional on the particular split occurring. This benchmark concerns fixed union membership; a data-selected descendant union loses the argument unless the union selection is accounted for. Substituting A's score or the realized Cover count would also lose this guarantee. For fixed k and fixed R²=r, the Beta upper-tail logarithm divided by n tends to `log(1−r)/2`, by the beta density and Laplace bounds at the lower integration endpoint. Thus the union-adjusted fixed-alpha critical value tends to R²=3/4, or rho=3. This gives a legitimate Gaussian conservative **threshold limit**, separate from a universal unimodality bound or B's noncentral reference.

The numerical check uses seed 20261109 and 12,000 iid Gaussian samples per panel. Fixed p=6 and spectrum-only rank give 136 and 120 rejections at nominal 0.01. Reusing the rank-specific Beta tail after the 90% observed contrast-energy rank rule gives 256/12,000, 95% exact binomial interval 0.01882–0.02408. This latter control uses the R² geometry and isolates the conditioning issue rather than executing the production kernel. The fixed-Gram Haar panel with an exact tied eigenblock gives 121/12,000. Exhaustive maxima over 127 unordered splits at n=8, k=2 give 131/12,000 with the exact all-label correction at nominal 0.01 (0.00914–0.01294). Strict flat-cut LPs independently find 28 cuts, including each maximizing partition, in all four checked samples. At ambient dimensions 7, 8 and 12 every split's full-span R² equals one to within 2e-15. For k=2 the all-label 0.01 critical rho falls from 4.886 at n=30 to 3.047 at n=900. These are finite panel checks of the exact result. ## Conditioning on the actual selection and preserving the score

The October 2026 Chen–Gao preprint gives a directly relevant same-data construction for an unknown shared feature covariance. Under its stronger Gaussian common-mean null for all rows in the tested union, it conditions on the union mean, outside observations, and centered union feature Gram. The remaining row orientation is Haar restricted to the event that the clustering returned the pair. Its general method replays the algorithm to check proposals, while its analytic shortcuts use specific linkage or k-means event constraints. The Besag–Clifford random-index rank is finite-sample valid under a reversible kernel on the restricted law and a path length and kernel fixed given the conditioning variables. Slow or disconnected movement can hurt power without changing that rank theorem. [Chen and Gao, Sections 3.3, 4.1 and 4.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.17458v1)

The rank proof also permits the current sibling score as the ranking function, provided it is deterministic from each replayed dataset and the conditioned quantities. This follows from the exchangeability of the observation index under the reversible path construction. Refit or fix every part of the score according to the original procedure and include all required selection in the event definition. Thus one can change the reference while retaining the sibling score, branch variance and projection rule. That is a mathematical extension of the generic rank argument, with no implementation or validation for the TBS diffusion/tree/NNLS/gate/traversal pipeline here. In particular the preprocessing defining Y must preserve the Gaussian row-orbit law or be included explicitly in the modeled replay, and conditional single-pair validity does not establish final-partition error control.

For the **global fixed-root** common-Gaussian-population null, an alternative exact reference avoids matching the observed pair. Condition on the observed feature Gram and mean. Draw B independent Haar row orientations, reconstruct datasets, and replay the complete root procedure, scoring the newly selected root split in each. The observation and replays are iid under the conditional orbit law, so `(1 + # replay scores >= observed score)/(B + 1)` is superuniform with ties and needs no covariance plug-in. A prespecified convention for replays without an eligible split is part of this statistic. This follows from the same Gaussian invariance but answers a different question than conditional selected-pair inference. It tests how extreme the procedure's root output is under the global null. The result can cover selection of the observed root score without requiring that every replay reproduce the same split, while retaining the cost of rebuilding the tree for each orientation. It does not transfer to selected descendants, selected-pair mean contrasts, or general non-Gaussian homogeneous populations. This option was derived here and is unimplemented for TBS.

## Eigenvalues, eigenspaces, and topology

Eigenvalues alone do not determine how individual cells project or which cuts are available. A repeated eigenvalue identifies its invariant subspace, within which individual eigenvectors can rotate. Keeping the entire tied block preserves basis-invariant projected distances and the fully maximized cut objective over that block. Selecting one vector inside the block can change both. An exact spectral tie, a numerical tolerance, a cell tied at a cut threshold, and two distinct nearly equally strong cuts are separate issues. With a positive external eigengap the block projector is stable under sufficiently small perturbation even when its internal eigenvectors move. An eigengap does not ensure separation in a sample or certify a population split. The number of retained dimensions can increase the attainable flat-cut count and raise score saturation without increasing the number of true populations. The MP edge is an estimated spectrum rule here. [Yu, Wang and Samworth, Davis–Kahan variant](https://arxiv.org/abs/1405.0680) · [Subtree spectrum evidence](subtree-spectrum-topology-20261108.md)

A hierarchy's clades form a laminar family. Two whole-set splits whose four intersections are nonempty cross, and they cannot both be represented as branches of one rooted binary hierarchy. This is a structural restriction independent of the p-value. Rotating within a tied eigenspace can reveal a crossing alternative, but an equal or near-equal projected score does not itself establish a second population or independent test. Counting branches of the realized hierarchy therefore does not account for every available alternative that the algorithm could have chosen.
