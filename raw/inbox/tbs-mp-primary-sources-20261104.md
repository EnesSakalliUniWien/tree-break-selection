# Primary sources for the Tree-Break Selection Marchenko–Pastur audit

Captured 2026-11-04. These are source-grounded notes about reference ensembles.

- [Marchenko and Pastur (1967), Distribution of eigenvalues for some sets of random matrices](https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/msb4101). The identity-population sample covariance specialization gives the limiting eigenvalue bulk with upper endpoint `sigma²(1+sqrt(d/n))²`, where `n` counts independent observations in the normalized scatter construction and `d/n` tends to a positive constant.
- [Johnstone (2001), On the distribution of the largest eigenvalue in principal components analysis](https://doi.org/10.1214/aos/1009210544). The Gaussian identity-Wishart top eigenvalue has a fluctuation law after centering and scaling. The centering term in Johnstone's approximation uses the number of *Wishart* degrees of freedom as its sample-size input. It is distinct from subtracting one degree of freedom for empirical centering and from a fixed-level quantile.
- [Baik and Silverstein, Eigenvalues of Large Sample Covariance Matrices of Spiked Population Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0408165). Under an identity bulk plus finitely many population spikes, whether an upper population spike separates in the sample limit depends on its strength and the aspect ratio. Counting above the sample bulk is a conditional signal-detection construction with a detectability boundary.
- [El Karoui (2009), Concentration of measure and spectra of random matrices](https://arxiv.org/abs/0912.1950). Under stated concentration and population assumptions, high-dimensional sample correlation and sample covariance spectra can be asymptotically similar. This supports considering diagonal normalization in specific large-sample models. Full same-row covariance whitening is a different transformation, and these results do not supply a selected-subtree finite-sample law.
