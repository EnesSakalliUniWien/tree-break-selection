# Primary references checked for the Tree-Break Selection knowledge audit

Checked 2026-11-04. These references support the mathematical scope below. Project-specific claims are in the audit report and cite their owning code and retained evidence.

- Marčenko and Pastur (1967), [Distribution of eigenvalues for some sets of random matrices](https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/msb4101). The classical identity-noise covariance model motivates the asymptotic upper bulk edge. Applying the edge to locally selected or estimated-whitened matrices is a separate modeling step.
- Johnstone (2001), [On the distribution of the largest eigenvalue in principal components analysis](https://doi.org/10.1214/aos/1009210544). The largest root of the identity Gaussian Wishart model has its own centered and scaled asymptotic distribution. The bulk boundary alone supplies neither a level-alpha spike detector nor a reference for a statistic calculated after selecting a PCA prefix.
- Benjamini and Yekutieli (2001), [The control of the false discovery rate in multiple testing under dependency](https://www.math.tau.ac.il/~ybenja/depApr27.pdf). Ordinary BH extends to the specified positive regression dependence condition on the true nulls. The paper supplies a different rule for arbitrary dependence. Neither result removes the requirement for valid null inputs.
- Yekutieli (2008), [Hierarchical false discovery rate-controlling methodology](https://www.math.tau.ac.il/~yekutiel/papers/JASA%20FDR%20trees.pdf). The analysis specifies a hierarchy of families, test dependence conditions, and which FDR is controlled. These are theorem-specific requirements.
- Bogomolov et al. (2021), [Hypotheses on a tree: new error rates and testing strategies](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9945647/). TreeBH provides guarantees for defined selective FDR measures at tree resolutions under theorem-specific conditions. A shared family-level recurrence does not establish a bound for a separate sibling correction or the final partition.
- Gao, Bien and Witten (2024), [Selective inference for hierarchical clustering](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.2022.2116331), [author preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.02936). Their Gaussian tests condition on clustering outcomes for the tested mean contrast and additional nuisance information under a specified model. Adapting that argument to adaptive diffusion, estimated branch lengths, fitted covariance/PCA/rank, and selected traversal requires establishing the law and the event used by each new test.
